What happened

Exit signs

What each dynamic sign shows right now in the live run. Signs are set by one search backward from a virtual exit node, looking 60 s ahead for smoke. A red sign means no safe way from that spot. People read a sign only if they can see it (visibility of at least 5 m).

Guidance layer

Sensors and live estimate

The routing never sees the real fire. It sees noisy readings from sensors that may be dead, stuck, reading too high or losing readings, plus phone reports from people who see smoke. From these it fits where the fire started and how fast smoke spreads, and predicts when smoke reaches every place, including places with no sensor.

How it works

The prototype follows the proposed approach in the seminar: four layers in a loop, the seven method steps, and one feature or more for each research gap found in the eight papers.

The four layers (repeated every Δt)

SensingSmoke, heat and CO sensors read every 5 s with noise and faults; camera counts give visitors; the building graph.
SituationRisk index and FED (ISO 13571); live prediction of smoke arrival; crowd density on each path.
DecisionFilter places above θ at the time you would reach them; time-dependent, congestion and dose aware routes; least-risk fallback or shelter; the commander confirms alerts.
GuidanceDynamic exit signs; phone messages; radio for staff; routes for rescue teams.

The seven method steps

1Model the building: places with area and capacity, paths with length and effective width.
2Read sensors and turn readings into a risk index [3] and FED [4].
3Predict hazard arrival from the spread rate [6], [7], fitted live.
4Filter places above θ; edge cost = travel time at density + α × exposure.
5Dijkstra to a virtual exit node; flow limits respected; most exposed groups planned first [4].
6Re-plan every Δt; keep γ-efficient alternative paths [8] and switch when jammed.
7Evaluate T50, T90, last person safe, dose and people reached by the hazard over repeated runs.

Limits of this prototype

  • The hospital, people, sensors, drills and recordings are made up. Nothing here is a measurement of a real building.
  • The fire and smoke are a simple spread model (graph distance, door factors, growth and decay), not a CFD fire model. Its constants are assumed.
  • People move as a network flow (SFPE speed and density), not as individual agents avoiding each other.
  • The live estimate assumes one fire origin and one spread speed; two separate fires would confuse it.
  • Calibration with one drill cannot separate walking speed from delay well; run-to-run spread is large.
  • Phone and sign behaviour (share of phones, compliance) are assumed values, to be replaced by survey or drill data.
This prototype supports planning, teaching and research. It does not replace a hospital's official emergency procedures or fire-safety professionals. Sources of every number are on the Parameters page.
